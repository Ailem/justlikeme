Why coverage matters
What the number is really protecting

Income protection

Replaces the earnings your household would lose, so everyday life can carry on.

Mortgage protection

Helps your family keep the home instead of being forced to sell or refinance.

Education funding

Keeps long-term goals like college on track even if an income disappears.

Debt protection

Covers balances like loans and cards so they don't become your family's burden.

Sources & terms

Actuarial methodology is the branch of math insurers use to measure risk and estimate how much coverage a household needs. LIMRA and Life Happens are non-profit research groups whose yearly Insurance Barometer Study tracks life-insurance ownership and the coverage gap. NAIC is the National Association of Insurance Commissioners — the group of U.S. state insurance regulators; its Life Insurance Buyer's Guide explains how to work out how much coverage you need.

What's behind the numbers: The underinsurance trend is published — about half of U.S. adults have life insurance and roughly 42% (about 102 million) say they need more (LIMRA / Life Happens, 2024). Your recommended range is a needs-based estimate — income replacement plus mortgage, education, debts and final expenses — the approach the NAIC Buyer's Guide describes. The "average for families like yours" figures are derived from LIMRA's Life Insurance Ownership in Focus (2016) — the most recent public per-group coverage study, shown in 2016 dollars, with an income adjustment from the same study (single-parent figures partly modeled). For scale: the average new individual policy purchased was $209,000 in 2024 (ACLI Fact Book), and the median coverage held per insured household was $150,000 in 2022 (Federal Reserve Survey of Consumer Finances, via ACLI).
